In her study of medieval trade records, historian Leila Mansour found that merchants' account books were far from ______: rather than recording every transaction, many merchants noted only unusually large sales, leaving most everyday exchanges undocumented.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
Aambiguous
BexhaustiveCorrect answer
CconventionalHis answer
Dobsolete

Why his answer is wrong

“Far from” reverses the meaning: the account books were the opposite of the missing word. The colon explains how: merchants did not record every transaction. So the books were far from complete. “Conventional” (ordinary) has no link to recording every transaction.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Read past the blank: “rather than recording every transaction … leaving most everyday exchanges undocumented.”
  2. Say what the books were in your own words: incomplete.
  3. “Far from ______” means the blank is the opposite: complete.
  4. The word meaning “complete, covering everything” is exhaustive.

Remember

With “far from,” “anything but,” “not,” or “rather than,” describe the text in your own words, flip it, then match a choice.

His answer: B · Correct: C

Urban red foxes tend to approach humans more readily than rural foxes do. Ecologist Daniel Osei hypothesizes that this boldness develops because urban foxes are regularly exposed to people as they grow up, rather than because naturally bold foxes are more likely to move into cities in the first place.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support Osei's hypothesis?
AUrban foxes are bolder than rural foxes in every city where the two groups have been compared.
BUrban foxes obtain a larger share of their food from human sources than rural foxes do.His answer
CCubs born to cautious parents in the city become bolder as they mature, while their siblings raised in rural areas remain cautious.Correct answer
DRural foxes that move into cities are already bolder than rural foxes that remain in the countryside.

Why his answer is wrong

The hypothesis compares two explanations: boldness comes from exposure to people, rather than from naturally bold foxes moving into cities. Choice B is only about diet. It doesn't help decide between the two explanations.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Name the two competing explanations: (1) exposure while growing up; (2) bold foxes choose to move to cities.
  2. The right evidence must favor (1) and rule out (2).
  3. Choice C keeps the parents the same (same inherited traits) and changes only where the cubs grow up. Only the city cubs become bold, so exposure is the cause.
  4. Choice A fits both explanations. Choice D supports explanation (2), the opposite of Osei's view.

Remember

When a claim says “X rather than Y,” look for the choice that supports X and rules out Y. A choice that fits both explanations doesn't support either one.

Information and Ideas · Inferences
His answer: B · Correct: C

Scholars have long debated whether an anonymous eighteenth-century pamphlet was written by the essayist Thomas Reade, whose other works it closely resembles in style. A recently discovered letter shows that the pamphlet was first printed in March 1752. Reade's diary, however, records that he was at sea from January through June of that year and had no way of sending manuscripts to printers. If both the letter and the diary are accurate, it is most reasonable to conclude that ______

Which choice most logically completes the text?
AReade's diary is an unreliable record of where he traveled.
BReade could not have been the author of the pamphlet.His answer
Cif Reade wrote the pamphlet, he completed it before his voyage began.Correct answer
Dthe pamphlet was printed without the knowledge of its author.

Why his answer is wrong

Choice B is too strong. The pamphlet was printed in March, but the text never says when it was written. Reade could have finished it before he sailed in January.

Step-by-step solution

  1. List the facts: printed March 1752; Reade at sea January–June 1752 and unable to send manuscripts.
  2. If he wrote it, he must have handed it over before January.

On inference questions, choose what must be true. Be suspicious of absolute words like “could not,” “always,” and “never.”

His answer: Left blank · Correct: D
In triangle \(ABC\), point \(D\) is on \(\overline{AB}\) and point \(E\) is on \(\overline{AC}\) such that \(\overline{DE}\parallel\overline{BC}\). If \(AD=4\), \(DB=8\), and \(DE=5\), what is the length of \(\overline{BC}\)?
His answerLeft blank
Correct answerD
A\(12.5\)
B\(20\)
C\(10\)
D\(15\)Correct answer

What this question tests

Left blank. Similar triangles and scaling were also missed on Exam 1 and Full SAT 1, so this is a recurring gap. Choice C (10) is the trap from using \(\frac{DB}{AD}\) instead of the whole side.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Since \(\overline{DE}\parallel\overline{BC}\), triangle \(ADE\) is similar to triangle \(ABC\).
  2. The whole side is \(AB=AD+DB=4+8=12\).

The scale factor always uses the whole side (AD + DB), never only the extra piece DB.

Math Module 2 · Question 3
Problem-Solving and Data Analysis · Conditional probability
His answer: 9/16 · Correct: 27/82
At a school, 45% of the students are juniors. Of the juniors, 30% own a bicycle. Of the students who are not juniors, 50% own a bicycle. If a student who owns a bicycle is selected at random, what is the probability that the student is a junior? (Express your answer as a fraction or a decimal.)
His answer9/16
Correct answer27/82

Why his answer is wrong

The denominator must be all bicycle owners. 9/16 equals \(0.45\div(0.30+0.50)\), which mixes the share of juniors with the two ownership rates.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Imagine 1,000 students: 450 juniors and 550 non-juniors.
  2. Juniors with bikes: \(0.30\times450=135\). Non-juniors with bikes: \(0.50\times550=275\).
  3. All bike owners: \(135+275=410\).
  4. \(P(\text{junior}\mid\text{bike})=\frac{135}{410}=\frac{27}{82}\approx0.329\).

Remember

For “of the students who…” probabilities, convert every percent into a count out of 1,000, then read the fraction from the counts.

Math Module 2 · Question 5
Geometry and Trigonometry · Arc length
His answer: A · Correct: B
A circle has center \(O\) and a radius of 12. Points \(A\) and \(B\) lie on the circle, and chord \(\overline{AB}\) has length 12. What is the length of minor arc \(AB\)?
A\(12\pi\)His answer
B\(4\pi\)Correct answer
C\(2\pi\)
D\(6\pi\)

Why his answer is wrong

\(12\pi\) is half the circumference (a 180° arc). The key fact was missed: a chord equal to the radius forms an equilateral triangle with the center.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Draw radii \(OA\) and \(OB\). Then \(OA=OB=12\) and \(AB=12\).
  2. Triangle \(OAB\) is equilateral, so the central angle is \(60^\circ=\frac{\pi}{3}\).
  3. Circumference \(=2\pi(12)=24\pi\).
  4. Arc \(=\frac{60}{360}\times24\pi=4\pi\) (or \(s=r\theta=12\cdot\frac{\pi}{3}\)).

Remember

Chord = radius → central angle 60°. Always draw the two radii to the chord's endpoints first.

Advanced Math · Sum and product of roots
His answer: Left blank · Correct: 38
The solutions to \(x^2-8x+13=0\) are \(r\) and \(s\). What is the value of \(r^2+s^2\)?
His answerLeft blank
Correct answer38

What this question tests

Left blank. This uses the sum and product of roots, which also appears in Q21: a new concept gap.

Step-by-step solution

  1. For \(ax^2+bx+c=0\): \(r+s=-\frac ba=8\) and \(rs=\frac ca=13\).
  2. Use the identity \(r^2+s^2=(r+s)^2-2rs\).
  3. \(r^2+s^2=8^2-2(13)=64-26=38\).

Remember

You don't need the roots themselves. Sum \(=-\frac ba\), product \(=\frac ca\), and \(r^2+s^2=(r+s)^2-2rs\).

Math Module 2 · Question 14
Advanced Math · Rational equations
His answer: A · Correct: D
\[\frac{x+2}{x-3}-\frac{5}{x-3}=4\] How many solutions does the equation above have?
AExactly oneHis answer
BInfinitely many
CExactly two
DZeroCorrect answer

Why his answer is wrong

Solving \(x+2-5=4(x-3)\) gives \(x=3\), but \(x=3\) makes the denominator 0, so it isn't allowed. It's the same mistake as the extraneous radical solution on Full SAT 1: a value found by algebra that fails the original equation.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Combine the fractions (same denominator): \(\frac{x+2-5}{x-3}=\frac{x-3}{x-3}\).
  2. For every \(x\neq3\), \(\frac{x-3}{x-3}=1\).
  3. So the equation says \(1=4\), which is never true.
  4. The only candidate, \(x=3\), is excluded. There are zero solutions.

Remember

Before answering, check every solution in the original equation. A value that makes a denominator 0 is never a solution.

His answer: C · Correct: D
The radius of a right circular cylinder is increased by 20%, and its height is decreased by 20%. How does the volume of the new cylinder compare with the volume of the original cylinder?
AIt is 4% greater.
BIt is the same.
CIt is 4% less.His answer
DIt is 15.2% greater.Correct answer

Why his answer is wrong

“4% less” comes from \(1.2\times0.8=0.96\), as if the radius appeared once. In \(V=\pi r^2h\), the radius is squared. This is the scaling concept again.

Step-by-step solution

  1. \(V=\pi r^2h\).
  2. New volume: \(\pi(1.2r)^2(0.8h)=1.44\times0.8\times\pi r^2h\).

  4. The new volume is 115.2% of the original: 15.2% greater.

Math Module 2 · Question 22
Algebra · Systems — infinitely many solutions
His answer: B · Correct: A
\[ax+by=12\]\[2x+8y=60\] In the system of equations above, \(a\) and \(b\) are constants. If the system has infinitely many solutions, what is the value of \(a+b\)?
A\(2\)Correct answer
B\(10\)His answer
C\(6\)
D\(\dfrac15\)

Why his answer is wrong

10 = 2 + 8: the coefficients of the second equation were copied without scaling. For infinitely many solutions, the two equations must be exact multiples of each other.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Infinitely many solutions means the first equation equals the second multiplied by one number.
  2. Constants: \(\frac{12}{60}=\frac15\).
  3. So \(a=2\times\frac15=\frac25\) and \(b=8\times\frac15=\frac85\).
  4. \(a+b=\frac{10}{5}=2\).

Remember

Find the multiplier from the constants first, then apply it to every coefficient.

What these mistakes have in common

  1. Re-read the last line. Is your number the quantity they asked for: b or b², k or x?
  2. Use every condition. “0 < k < 5,” “positive,” and “x ≠ 3” are there to rule out answers.
  3. Check solutions in the original equation. A value that makes a denominator 0 is never a solution.
  4. Scaling: lengths × k, areas × k², volumes × k³. In a formula, apply each change with its exponent.
  5. Sum and product of roots: sum = −b/a, product = c/a.
