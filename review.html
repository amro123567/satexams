Four habits for the next test

  1. Re-read the last line before choosing. Ask: "Is my number what they asked for?" (\(x\) or \(k\)? per hour or per 6 hours? \(AB\) or \(DB\)?)
  2. Write substituted values in brackets. \((-1)^3\), \(x-(-4)\). One extra line prevents most sign errors.
  3. If the exponent is not just \(t\), rewrite it. \(b^{t/6}=(b^{1/6})^t\) and \(b^{2t}=(b^2)^t\).
  4. Never leave a blank. With 2 minutes left, fill every empty answer.

Part A · M1 Q7

Similar triangles: a parallel line makes a small copy

In M1 Q7, \(\overline{DE}\parallel\overline{BC}\), \(AD=6\), \(DB=9\), and the area of triangle \(ADE\) is 24. The question asks for the area of quadrilateral \(DBCE\).

Step 1 · Why the triangles are similar

A line parallel to one side creates equal corresponding angles: \(\angle ADE=\angle ABC\) and \(\angle AED=\angle ACB\), and both triangles share \(\angle A\). Equal angles mean similar triangles (AA similarity): \(\triangle ADE\) is a scaled-down copy of \(\triangle ABC\).

Step 2 · Find the scale factor from matching sides

Match each side of the small triangle with the same side of the big one. \(AD\) matches \(AB\), the whole side, not \(DB\).

\(k=\dfrac{AD}{AB}=\dfrac{6}{6+9}=\dfrac{6}{15}=\dfrac25\)

Step 3 · Areas scale by the square

When every length is multiplied by \(k\), the area is multiplied by \(k^2\). The pictures show why: doubling the sides makes 4 copies of the small triangle, and tripling makes 9.

M1 Q7 (\(k=\frac25\))\(\times\frac25\)\(\times\frac4{25}\)—

Step 4 · Finish

\(\dfrac{\text{Area }ADE}{\text{Area }ABC}=\dfrac{4}{25}\Rightarrow \text{Area }ABC=24\cdot\dfrac{25}{4}=150\). The quadrilateral is the big triangle minus the small one: \(150-24=\mathbf{126}\).

Traps the choices were built on
  • \(k=\frac69\) (using \(DB\) instead of \(AB\)): wrong scale factor.
  • 54: using \(k=\frac25\) on the area without squaring it.
  • 150: the whole triangle; you forgot to subtract the small one.
Recipe for every "parallel line in a triangle" question: (1) find matching sides through the shared vertex, (2) \(k=\frac{\text{small}}{\text{whole}}\), (3) lengths \(\times k\), areas \(\times k^2\), (4) subtract if the question asks for the leftover piece.

Part B · M2 Q6

Right-triangle trig: every ratio depends on where you stand

In M2 Q6, angle \(C\) is the right angle and \(\sin A=\frac{5}{13}\). The question asks for \(\tan A+\tan B\).

Step 1 · SOH-CAH-TOA

\(\sin=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\qquad \cos=\dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\qquad \tan=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\)

Step 2 · Turn the ratio into a triangle

\(\sin A=\frac{5}{13}\) means: the side opposite \(A\) is 5 and the hypotenuse is 13. The Pythagorean theorem gives the third side: \(\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=\sqrt{144}=12\).

Step 3 · Read each angle from its own corner

The hypotenuse never changes, but opposite and adjacent swap when you move from \(A\) to \(B\).

From \(A\): \(\tan A=\frac{5}{12}\). From \(B\): \(\tan B=\frac{12}{5}\).

Step 4 · Finish

\(\tan A+\tan B=\dfrac{5}{12}+\dfrac{12}{5}=\dfrac{25+144}{60}=\mathbf{\dfrac{169}{60}}\)

Facts that save time (when \(C=90^\circ\), so \(A+B=90^\circ\)):
• \(\sin A=\cos B\) and \(\cos A=\sin B\)
• \(\tan B=\dfrac{1}{\tan A}\), so \(\tan A\cdot\tan B=1\)
• Shortcut: \(\tan A+\tan B=\dfrac{c^2}{ab}=\dfrac{13^2}{5\cdot12}=\dfrac{169}{60}\)
• Know the triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25 and their multiples
Traps the choices were built on
  • \(\frac{60}{169}\): the right answer flipped.
  • 1: that is \(\tan A\times\tan B\), not the sum.
  • Reading \(\tan B\) from angle \(A\)'s point of view, which gives \(\frac{5}{12}\) twice.
